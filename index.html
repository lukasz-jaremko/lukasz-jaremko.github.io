Approach

The states that govern molecular function and ligand response.

AI-based structure prediction, cryo-EM and crystallography now provide structural models for far more proteins and assemblies than before. The next questions are dynamic: which states are populated in solution, how they interconvert, how interactions, mutations and post-translational modifications reshape them, and how ligands alter function. NMR is central because it measures these processes directly, including transient, low-population and structurally heterogeneous states and cryptic binding sites that static models cannot fully describe.

I develop and apply advanced NMR to enzyme mechanism and inhibition, chromatin regulation, protein interactions and molecular discovery. A major focus is regulatory proteins and regions that remain difficult to capture structurally, including intrinsically disordered proteins and regions, dynamic monomers and oligomers, and proteins moving between soluble, phase-separated, assembled and aggregated states. Integrated with quantitative solution biophysics, X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, biochemical validation and computational structural models, NMR connects structure with motion, recognition and regulation and identifies states that can be inhibited, stabilized, redirected or rescued. The same NMR capabilities translate directly into molecular discovery, from fragment screening and chemical-probe optimization to drug-candidate development, metabolomics, natural products and complex mixtures.

Research

From molecular mechanism to chemical intervention.

Chromatin regulation & epigenetic enzymes

I define how NSD-family methyltransferases and nucleosome complexes move between regulatory states, how cancer-associated mutations and regulatory interactions alter activity, and which states can be selectively targeted by small molecules.

Protein dynamics & molecular recognition

I measure conformational exchange and transient protein–protein and protein–ligand interactions to identify regulatory states and cryptic sites that are difficult to infer from a dominant static structure.

IDPs, disorder, aggregation & heterogeneous states

In intrinsically disordered proteins such as α-synuclein, I resolve early association, metal and ligand interactions, and heterogeneous states relevant to neurodegeneration that determine aggregation, assembly and ligand response.

NMR dynamics & enhanced detection

I develop relaxation, exchange, diffusion, direct-detection, selective-labeling, ligand-observed and multinuclear NMR methods to quantify dynamics and reveal weak, transient and low-population molecular states.

Integrated structural mechanisms

I combine NMR with X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, quantitative biophysics, biochemistry and computational structural models to test structural hypotheses and connect molecular state with biochemical function.

Chemical discovery, natural products & metabolomics

Protein- and ligand-observed NMR and fragment screening validate binding, map ligand sites and support probe optimization; metabolomics extends the same analytical rigor to natural products and complex biological samples.

Scientific leadership

Building shared platforms, collaborations and research programs.

Since 2017, I have led independent research groups and university-wide initiatives that combine research programs, shared infrastructure and external funding. I led acquisition of a new 800 MHz high-field NMR platform and modernization of 700 and 950 MHz systems. TCI, TXO and QCI-F cryogenic configurations added sensitive 1H detection, direct 13C and 15N detection and biomolecular 19F capabilities; 3.2 mm E-free MAS extended the platform to membrane proteins, oligomers, large assemblies and aggregates. Automation and an NMR-based metabolomics workflow opened the platform to higher-throughput and complex biological samples.

These investments were developed as shared platforms to expand access to advanced NMR and structural methods, support new projects across departments, and connect university researchers with hospitals, medical schools, research institutes, startups and companies. Joint projects and hands-on training broadened the user base, trained new users and led to new collaborations, funding, research directions and publications.

I also co-lead university-wide expansion and modernization of shared research facilities, including scientific planning for an NIH C06 proposal, and have served in faculty recruitment, evaluation and steering of core facilities and multi-million-dollar infrastructure. Shared infrastructure is most valuable when it enables more experiments, lets researchers test new ideas, opens funding opportunities, strengthens collaborations and expands institutional research capacity.

Teaching & Training

Teaching science through measurement, mechanism and application.

I have taught undergraduate and graduate courses spanning mathematics, NMR spectroscopy, EPR, biophysical chemistry and advanced molecular instrumentation. My teaching connects quantitative principles with real measurements: how signals arise, what an experiment can resolve, how to choose an appropriate experiment, and how data become mechanistic understanding in structural biology and molecular discovery.

Hands-on work with research instruments and real datasets prepares students for both research and technology-driven careers. Former trainees have progressed to independent academic research and to scientific roles in industry, analytical and regulatory science, and core facilities. I want students to connect theory with experiment and molecular measurement with mechanism and application.
