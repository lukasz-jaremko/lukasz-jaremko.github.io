Approach

Structural motions and molecular interactions: deciphering mechanisms for precise chemical modulation.

My work bridges biophysical chemistry, chemical physics and chemical biology, with biomolecular NMR at the experimental core. I approach biomolecules as dynamic systems whose function emerges from structure, populations, interactions, kinetics and transitions between states. Structural models become most informative when they are connected to experimentally measured molecular behavior.

AI-based structure prediction, cryo-EM and crystallography now provide increasingly detailed structural models. NMR measures what those models do in solution: molecular mobility, conformational populations and exchange, transient protein–protein and protein–ligand interactions, and the effects of mutations, post-translational modifications and small molecules. I use these measurements from enzyme regulation and atomic interactions through fragment screening, small-molecule inhibition, and optimization of chemical probes and drug candidates. The same NMR capabilities extend to metabolites, natural products and complex mixtures, where composition, structure and interactions must be determined experimentally. I combine NMR with X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, quantitative solution biophysics, biochemistry and cellular experiments, and work with computational collaborators to relate experimental observables to structural ensembles and mechanism.

Research

Structural biology of protein dynamics, molecular recognition and chemical intervention.

Chromatin regulation & epigenetic enzymes

NSD-family methyltransferases, ASH1L, nucleosome recognition and catalytic regulation. I study how molecular interactions, conformational states and disease-associated changes shape chromatin-enzyme function and create opportunities for selective chemical intervention.

Protein dynamics & molecular recognition

Many binding sites and regulatory states are transient rather than fixed. I use NMR and quantitative biophysics to connect conformational exchange with protein–protein and protein–ligand recognition, including weak and cryptic interactions that are difficult to define from static structures alone.

Disorder, aggregation & heterogeneous states

Work on α-synuclein and other aggregation-prone systems addresses molecular states that are intrinsically heterogeneous. The focus is on transient contacts, metal and ligand interactions, early association events and the biophysical chemistry that connects molecular ensembles with aggregation behavior.

NMR dynamics & enhanced detection

I develop and apply relaxation, exchange, diffusion, selective-labeling, ligand-observed and multinuclear NMR approaches to improve quantitative measurements of dynamics and the detection of weak, transient, low-abundance and heterogeneous molecular species.

NMR integrated with structural biology

I combine NMR with X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, quantitative solution biophysics and biochemical experiments, and work with computational collaborators to relate experimental observables to structural ensembles. The aim is a mechanistic model supported by independent measurements of the same molecular system.

Chemical discovery, natural products & metabolomics

Protein- and ligand-observed NMR, fragment screening, molecular probes and complex-mixture analysis connect structural biology with chemical discovery. The same expertise extends to natural products, metabolites and chemically complex samples where molecular identity, interaction and function must be resolved experimentally.

Scientific leadership

Research programs, shared infrastructure and scientific community.

Since 2017, I have led independent research groups, multidisciplinary collaborations and major NMR modernization efforts, including acquisition and implementation of a new 800 MHz high-field NMR platform and modernization of 700 and 950 MHz systems. The resulting platform joined solution-state biomolecular NMR, solid-state MAS, quantitative solution biophysics and automated molecular discovery. TCI, TXO and QCI-F cryogenic configurations enabled high-sensitivity proton-detected experiments, direct low-γ 13C and 15N detection, and biomolecular 19F applications; a 3.2 mm E-free MAS capability extended the platform to membrane proteins, oligomers, large assemblies and aggregates. SampleJet automation increased throughput, and I introduced an NMR-based metabolomics workflow for complex biological samples.

The modernization was designed as a shared scientific platform across the university, linking NMR with crystallography, cryo-EM, quantitative biophysics, biochemistry and computational structural biology through collaborations. It broadened the user base across biosciences, biochemistry, plant sciences and environmental research, and helped build research collaborations with hospitals, medical schools and biomedical groups beyond the university. Shared projects and hands-on training spread advanced NMR expertise to new users and faculty collaborators and generated new collaborations, funding opportunities, research directions and publications.

I am also involved in and co-lead university-wide efforts to expand, relocate and modernize NMR capabilities as a shared platform for high-field biomolecular NMR, integrated structural biology, solution biophysics, computational structural biology, biochemistry and translational research. This work includes scientific planning for an NIH C06 research-facility proposal and coordination with research leadership, architects, facilities teams and scientific stakeholders to broaden capabilities, access and the research community that can grow around them.

Since 2017, I have participated in faculty recruitment and evaluation across biosciences, structural biology and related molecular sciences. I see shared infrastructure, strategic recruitment and collaborative program development as interconnected parts of building a durable research environment: strengthening existing investigators, attracting complementary expertise, and creating new cross-disciplinary projects that grow across laboratories and departments.

Teaching & Training

From quantitative principles to modern molecular technology.

I have taught undergraduate and graduate courses spanning mathematics, NMR spectroscopy, EPR, biophysical chemistry and advanced molecular instrumentation. My teaching connects quantitative principles with real measurements: how signals arise, what an experiment can resolve, how to choose the right technology, and how to turn data into mechanistic understanding in structural biology and molecular discovery.

Hands-on work with research instruments and real datasets prepares students for both research and technology-driven careers. Former trainees have progressed to independent academic research and to scientific roles in industry, analytical and regulatory science, and core facilities. The goal is to train scientists who can move confidently from theory to experiment, and from molecular measurement to mechanism and application.
